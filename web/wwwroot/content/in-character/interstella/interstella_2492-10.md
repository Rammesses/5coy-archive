### The Search For Alien Life.

A REPORT BY PROF. B. M.
BARNACAL OF EARTH.

Human-kind has for many thousands of
years wondered if in this infinitely large
universe they are unique and alone.
Before the availability of powerful
telescopes, man assumed that
humanoid/alien races lived on most
planets, including those in Earth's own
solar system - specifically MARS and
the MOON. After this fanciful notion
had been discounted, man started to
search the stars using huge radio
receivers, unfortunately, due to the
speed of light, all that this discovered
were more stars and galaxies, and no
transmitted radio noise or
communications.

Throughout the late 20th and early 21st
centuries, there were numerous
sightings of 'unidentified flying objects'
(UFOs) - but these were mostly
dismissed as freaks of weather, meteors
burning up in the atmosphere and
aircraft testing. Eventually sightings
became rare.

With the advent of space travel within
Earth's solar system, scientists and
intelligent thinking men started to
assume that any life outside Earth
would be a very long way off, and
therefore probably never contactable.
Interest died down until the Jump
Synchronicity Enabler (JSE) was
developed. As it became possible to
traverse the great distances between
planets, the scientists said that "if there
is life we will find it", to which some
politicians replied "do we want to find
life? and if we find it how shall we use
it? and if we cannot use it how shall
we destroy it?"

So colonisation began apace, humans
and human derivatives have established
themselves on many planets, each with
their own governments and populations
of millions. In all of this, there has
never been a single shred of evidence
to allow for the possibility of alien life
forms.

It is therefore my opinion, and the
opinion of many learned colleagues,
that humans were a freak accident in
the development of a lone planet, and
that we are truely alone in this very
large universe.

All correspondance should be
transmitted to:

The Planetary Research Institute,

Alien Lifeform Division,
