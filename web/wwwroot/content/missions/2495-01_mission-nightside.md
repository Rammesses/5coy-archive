Grant's World was meant to be a quiet one. A marginal Class M planet, it was being made ready by a UW-funded colony prep team of 10 as a new home for Groombridge refugees. Then their equipment started to disappear, and then their people, until half the team were dead or missing. The Colonial Survey's investigators blamed the colonists and left, and their shuttle vanished from the Lone Star's sensors in mid-climb, with a last transmission of "My God what's that... the light, the light". In January 2495 Major Harris took 5 Coy down under cover of a search-and-rescue training mission. Before the drop, Sgt Turner gave the company its first radio operator's course, and the new Surgeon Commander Mimiannapandawawa ("you can call me Ma'am") introduced the IR monitoring cards that would follow the Marines from then on.

Grant's World was odd from the start. There were patches of white fog in bright sunshine, and a masked figure in black with a limp who couldn't be caught. Rings of white powder, once analysed, turned out to be metal, plastic and animal tissue reduced to their component molecules with a strong magnetic charge: as far as anyone could tell, all that was left of the shuttle and its passengers. The hostiles were Hengists, a cult convinced that friendly aliens were coming to save humanity, who wanted the colonists gone before they arrived. One of the colony team turned out to be feeding them. Two black androids, the Soldier and the Jester, came and went with a whistle and a bang. A patrol found a frozen body carrying the notes of an expedition to "XB242", whose members matched the original archaeology team sent to X244. Then Farquhar dropped in, as urbane as ever, to offer the Marines a free ticket off the planet. Mexal walked him 200m out of the base and handed back his sidearm, and he disappeared in a whistle, a red flash and a circle of white dust.

The raid on the Hengist camp didn't go to plan. Capt Zwalf's squad was taken down in 30 seconds flat, and the company came back to find a bomb in the base: a modified CMC area-effect device with a timer and anti-tamper wiring. Marines Cardinal and Beck carried it 300m out, and Cardinal and Mexal blew the detonator off it in the morning, only to find the drum was practically empty. One of the missing colonists then staggered in, babbling about "men with stars in their eyes", "the big pink" and a circle that summoned them if you ran through it. The trail led to a gully where Marines Wellesley and Riccotonini lay inside another circle. Someone dragged Riccotonini out, and Zwalf charged in for Wellesley. There was a whistle, a flash and a bang, and when the smoke cleared both of them had gone. Mexal's samples of the dust afterwards showed, to everyone's relief, no trace of Marine.

Calvin's [report](/article/mission-reports/2495-01_mission-nightside) concluded that someone was using teleportation, or faking it, perhaps the same equipment he'd reported being sold illegally on Earth a few months before. He also warned it might be a stunt, like the Boeing-McDonnell Douglas JSE hoax of 2183. [Mexal's letter](/article/mexals-letters/mexals-letters_2495-01_grants-world) suspected a working matter transporter, with the Hengists as gullible cover. Calvin's guess that "we may yet see Capt. Zwalf and Marine Welesley again" turned out right. Zwalf was back in command for [Groombridge](/mission/2495-04_mission-unknown) in April, though far from his right mind, and Wellesley had returned by the time of the [Marie Celesta](/mission/2495-05_boarding-action-mariecelesta). Farquhar didn't stay gone either, and was back to his old tricks on [Mission 'Red Storm'](/mission/2496-03_mission-redstorm). The [honours list](/article/out-of-character/honours_1995-08-23) decorated Beck and Cardinal for removing the bomb, and Zwalf for going into the circle after Wellesley.
