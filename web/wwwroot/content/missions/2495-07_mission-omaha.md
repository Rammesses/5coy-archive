5 Coy went back to Hal. A year earlier, on [Mission 'Ogre'](/mission/2494-04_mission-ogre), UW Intelligence had sent the company in to help a government minister crush a slave revolt, on his promise that he'd then abolish slavery. He was lying, and the company ended up surrendering to the Hal army. Now the UW had decided to back the rebellion openly. In July 2495, Maj Zwalf briefed a short-handed squad of 13 Marines under Acting Captain Calvin. The job was to escort 3 CMC covert operations officers, Lts Churchill, Winters and Knapp, with their training kit to the 3 rebel groups: the Freemen For Freedom, the Hal Freedom Army and the Slaves For Freedom. They were also to bring back the data the rebels had promised the UW. Orders on Hal regulars were to neutralise them "with extreme prejudice", which Zwalf helpfully clarified as "kill them".

Day 1 went sideways almost immediately. The squad took in a beaten runaway slave they named Harry. When Slave Security turned up looking for him, Calvin and T/Sgt Mexal shot 2 of them, just before Cpl Martins handed over a note from their leader: "Hello V Corp., I'm CIA let us go through." Oops. The rebel groups trusted each other even less than they trusted the Marines. The SFF delegation gunned down 6 slaves who recognised them and might talk. By evening the camp had been hit by Hal regulars, the friendly CIA man was warning that 100+ more were on their way, and the squad was abandoning kit in the dark as an APC rolled into the field. They still found time to storm a hilltop outpost and rescue the SFF's captured radio men.

Day 2 was a running fight through the woods, and the Hal army was clearly hunting rebel cells one at a time. Harry turned up dead in a 49th Unified fire team, so he'd been an infiltrator all along. Lt Churchill was killed in the crossfire of a rescue attempt. Most of the squad went down under heavy fire, then Cyber Knight wandered in with an FFF contact, more than a year after the company had left him behind on Ogre. Sgt Turner debriefed him by data download and later found the corrupted interface protocols behind his misbehaving cyberhand. With all 3 data disks in hand, word came that Knapp and Winters had been captured. Oslo argued for getting the data off planet, but the squad went in anyway, after a misplotted grid reference and a move of the pickup to the G2's 18:50 radar gap. They found both officers drugged and fresh from "confessing" on video. In the final stand on the extraction hill, Turner called for covering fire and dragged a downed Mexal back up the slope by the scruff of the neck as the dropship landed.

On paper, and in Calvin's [mission report](/article/mission-reports/2495-07_mission-omaha), it was a clean sweep: equipment delivered, data recovered, 2 of the 3 officers brought home. Schwartzkopf was made Marine First Class, and Turner, Doe, Krinly and Mexal passed their Med-Tech 4 on the way back, sitting the exam on the Garavaglia. The truth was messier. The operation had been blown before the G2 even reached orbit, the rebels were riddled with government plants, and Hal still held a confession tape that could embarrass the UW. Calvin was glad of the chance to pay the Hal army back for 2494. [Mexal wondered](/article/mexals-letters/mexals-letters_2495-07_hal) whether the UW should have been interfering at all. [In the Line of Fire's year-end round-up](/article/in-the-line-of-fire/itlof_2496-01) was more concerned with setting the record straight: 5 Coy had left only 47 bodies on Hal, "not over 50".
