5 Coy went back to Hal. A year earlier, on [Mission 'Ogre'](/mission/2494-04_mission-ogre), UW Intelligence had sent the company in to help a government minister crush a slave revolt, on his promise that he'd then abolish slavery. He was lying, and the company ended up surrendering to the Hal army. Now the UW had decided to back the rebellion openly. In July 2495, Maj Zwalf briefed a short-handed squad of 13 Marines under Acting Captain Calvin. The job was to escort 3 CMC covert operations officers, Lts Churchill, Winters and Knapp, with their training kit to the 3 rebel groups, and to bring back the data the rebels had promised the UW. Orders on Hal regulars were to neutralise them "with extreme prejudice", which Zwalf helpfully clarified as "kill them".

It went sideways almost immediately. The squad took in a beaten runaway slave they named Harry. When Slave Security came looking for him, Calvin and T/Sgt Mexal shot 2 of them, just before Cpl Martins handed over a note from their leader: "Hello V Corp., I'm CIA let us go through." Oops. By nightfall the camp was blown, and the squad was abandoning kit in the dark as an APC rolled into the field. From then on it was a running fight through the woods, with the Hal army hunting the rebel cells one at a time. Harry turned up dead in a Hal army fire team, so he'd been an infiltrator all along, and Lt Churchill was killed in the crossfire of a rescue attempt. Cyber Knight wandered in, more than a year after the company had left him behind on Ogre. Then, with all 3 data disks in hand, word came that Knapp and Winters had been captured. The squad went in anyway and found both of them drugged and fresh from "confessing" on video. It finished with a stand on the extraction hill, where Sgt Turner dragged a downed Mexal back up the slope by the scruff of the neck as the dropship landed.

On paper, and in Calvin's [mission report](/article/mission-reports/2495-07_mission-omaha), it was a clean sweep: equipment delivered, data recovered, 2 of the 3 officers brought home. The truth was messier. The operation had been blown before the G2 even reached orbit, the rebels were riddled with government plants, and Hal still held a confession tape that could embarrass the UW. Calvin was glad of the chance to pay the Hal army back for 2494. [Mexal wondered](/article/mexals-letters/mexals-letters_2495-07_hal) whether the UW should have been interfering at all. [In the Line of Fire's year-end round-up](/article/in-the-line-of-fire/itlof_2496-01) was more concerned with setting the record straight: 5 Coy had left only 47 bodies on Hal, "not over 50".
