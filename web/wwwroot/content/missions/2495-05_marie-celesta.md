This one wasn't a mission so much as an ambush. [Welcome to the Marie Celesta](/article/scenarios/mission-welcome-to-the-marie-celesta) was written as part of the Drive Wars sequence: an inter-mission event that would teach the players about FTL travel and its problems without them noticing. Its props included working computer programs, and its brief was "ALIEN, with just a little Dark Star". The Garavaglia II was on a dull transit, with the Marines bored stiff, when the ship dropped out of jump almost on top of a dead freighter with life signs aboard. With no dropships and next to no kit, 20 Marines went across the umbilical with sidearms, screens and chemical light sticks. Torches and radios were banned because of a passive security system that reacted to anything electrical.

Inside it was dark, with bodies hacked to death by the airlock. The survivors were mad to varying degrees, from a man who'd lost his "poodle" to a self-styled crime lord called "Tech", running a harem and sitting on the medical supplies. T/Sgt Mexal [spent most of the weekend](/article/mexals-letters/mexals-letters_2495-05_mariecelesta) on the terminals, which was exactly what the programs were there for, and that was the trap. Bringing the network back up woke the anti-collision system, which ripped the umbilical away. Poking at the navigation then loaded a corrupted jump plot: 625 light years at a DF of 0.86, with every change locked out. "Tech" turned out to be E.K. Arnold, the ship's design engineer, but even with his codes and those of Hadens-Worthy executive Ms Housonens, nobody could stop the jump. The party fought its way to the only working EEV, with 20 Marines and 3 civilians in a pod rated for 12, and launched 42 seconds before the Marie Celesta blew itself apart. Sgt Trader, Acting Cpl S.C. Ratch and Marine Jarvis [didn't make it out](/article/mission-reports/2495-05_boarding-report-mariecelesta). All 3 were decorated posthumously in the [August 1995 honours](/article/out-of-character/honours_1995-08-23).

The fallout went on for much longer than the weekend. [In the Line of Fire](/article/in-the-line-of-fire/itlof_2495-06) reported 264 lost, and the UW inquiry [kept on not reporting](/article/in-the-line-of-fire/itlof_2495-12). CNN were still chasing the story when they turned up on [Mission 'Nova'](/mission/2495-11_mission-nova), and Lt Oslo was [shot on his way to give evidence](/article/out-of-character/interim-history_2496-to-2501). The intercepted Hadens-Worthy signal filed with Mexal's letter shows what was really going on. The ship was a testbed for their A-23 jump-drive project, the "insanity" was massive jump trauma, and they wanted Arnold and Housonens back before anyone else talked to them.
