This one wasn't a mission so much as an ambush. [Welcome to the Marie Celesta](/article/scenarios/mission-welcome-to-the-marie-celesta) was written as part of the Drive Wars sequence: an inter-mission event that would teach the players something about FTL travel and its problems without them noticing. Its props included working computer programs for the players to wrestle with. The brief was "ALIEN, with just a little Dark Star". The Garavaglia II is on a dull transit. The Marines are bored stiff, playing cards and putting up with Lt Oslo's Tactical What-ifs. Then the ship drops out of jump almost on top of a dead freighter with life signs aboard. With no dropships and next to no kit, 20 Marines went across the umbilical with sidearms, screens, a rat pack each and chemical light sticks. Torches and radios were banned because of a passive security system that reacted to anything electrical.

What they found was not good. The interior was dark, there were bodies hacked to death near the airlock, and a lot of the ship was open to vacuum or full of hard radiation. The surviving passengers and crew were mad to varying degrees. One wanted the sauna fixed, one had lost his "poodle", and a pair were out hunting rabbits. In amongst them were hostile mercenaries, a security robot that shot at anything that moved, and a self-styled crime lord called "Tech" running a harem and sitting on the medical supplies. The ship's clock also thought it was August 2494. T/Sgt Mexal [spent most of the weekend](/article/mexals-letters/mexals-letters_2495-05_mariecelesta) on the terminals, which was exactly what the programs were there for. Bring the network back up and the anti-collision system wakes up and rips the umbilical away. Poke at the navigation and the system loads a corrupted jump plot from backup: 625 light years at a DF of 0.86, with every change locked out. Spot the mistake? Rebooting the computers was the obvious thing to do, and it was also the trap.

From there it was a race against the JSE synch countdown. "Tech" turned out to be E.K. Arnold, the ship's design engineer. Between his codes, an experimental mood-altering drug from the cargo manifest and Hadens-Worthy executive Ms Housonens, the party disabled collision avoidance but still couldn't touch the jump. As the final minutes ticked away, they fought their way to the only working EEV. 20 Marines and 3 civilians went into a pod rated for 12, and it launched 42 seconds before the Marie Celesta blew itself apart. Sgt Trader, Acting Cpl S.C. Ratch and Marine Jarvis [didn't make it out](/article/mission-reports/2495-05_boarding-report-mariecelesta). All 3 were decorated posthumously in the [August 1995 honours](/article/out-of-character/honours_1995-08-23) "for laying down their lives that others might be saved".

The fallout went on for a lot longer than the weekend. [In the Line of Fire](/article/in-the-line-of-fire/itlof_2495-06) reported 264 lost, a suitcase of forged credits and some awkward experimental CMC weapons found aboard. CNN were still chasing interviews about her last hours when they turned up on [Mission 'Nova'](/mission/2495-11_mission-nova). The UW inquiry [kept on not reporting](/article/in-the-line-of-fire/itlof_2495-12), and Lt Oslo was [shot on his way to give evidence](/article/out-of-character/interim-history_2496-to-2501). The intercepted Hadens-Worthy signal filed with Mexal's letter shows what was really going on: the ship was a testbed for their A-23 jump-drive project, the "insanity" was massive jump trauma, and they wanted Arnold and Housonens back before anyone else talked to them. Mexal's theory of failed IR shielding was a good guess, but it was wrong.
